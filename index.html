Letters from
Kraków

1934 – 1939

Twenty-four letters, postcards, and documents exchanged between my maternal grandfather Albert (Alku) Wachtel in New York and his family back in Krakow. Albert died in 1964 when I was 4 years old so unfortunatly I don't have any memories of him. I believe I got the letters from my mom but don't remember the circumstances. In 2014 I scanned them on a flatbed scanner. (Could just use my phone now). These are all I have... its possible I can find more in my mom's stuff but no idea really. These cover the years 1934-39.

The Wachtel Family

A family in Kraków,
a son in New York

Albert (Alku) Wachtel emigrated from Kraków to New York in June 1936, aboard the Manhattan. His sisters, stepmother, and extended family remained behind. For five years they wrote to him — about illness and matchmaking, photographs that never arrived, parcels of American clothing received with deep gratitude, and, quietly, the growing shadow of what was coming.

Albert Wachtel

Born October 14, 1907 in Kraków. Emigrated to New York, June 1936. Married to Sarah (Sala) Schaeffer. Father of Reva (Rywusia, b. 1937) and Bessie (b. 1939). He is the recipient of almost all the letters in this collection. In his later years he wrote a brief archival note indexing the letters: "Helen, Rosa, Ida, brothers killed by Nazis."

The Family in Kraków

Albert's full sisters Rosa (Rózia) and Ida remained in Kraków. His half-sisters Helena (Hela), Andzia (Sydonia), and Regina also wrote. His father Lable Wachtel and stepmother Sidonia Fingerhut were both killed by the Nazis. The Fingerhut and Klapwald families appear throughout the correspondence.

A Complicated Family

Albert's father Lable married twice — both times to sisters from the Fingerhut family. His first wife Riva (Albert's mother) died young. He then married her younger sister Sidonia — making Albert's half-siblings simultaneously his first cousins once removed. This explains why the Fingerhut name appears so warmly throughout the letters.

The Last Letter

The final personal letter in the collection is dated July 21, 1939 — six weeks before Germany invaded Poland on September 1, 1939. Andzia writes: "There is simply no way to get to America. We who remain here are now protected only by hope." The Certificate of Poverty for Anna Wachtel was issued on August 15, 1939 — seventeen days before the invasion.
